Slide 2 Proposed Solution (Megh-Drishti: The Big Picture)
Target Time: 75 Seconds
The Story Angle: The "Camera Lens" Analogy
Explain the 12 km grid defect simply: a blurred camera photo that needs a smart high-definition zoom lens.
Spoken Script (Word-for-Word)
"To understand why forecasts fail us today, imagine looking at a photo that has been blurred. Current global weather models run on a 12 km grid. In simple terms, a single 12 km box covers 144 square kilometers. If an intense storm hits a small village, the model averages it across the entire 144 km area. The peak disappears, the warning stays green or yellow, and disaster strikes unprepared.

Megh-Drishti solves this with a Two-Stage AI approach that acts like a smart zoom lens:
• First: Our 4D Spatio-Temporal Tracker watches the atmosphere on a 3D spherical mesh. It calculates an Extreme Forecast Index (EFI) to spot anomalies 3 to 10 days ahead, tracking where the storm is headed with an expanding confidence cone.
• Second: Once an anomaly is detected, our Generative Diffusion Model (DDPM) zooms into that specific danger zone, sharpening the forecast from 12 km down to 5 km—recovering the extreme rainfall peaks that normal models smooth out.
• Finally: This feeds into an interactive GIS Command Centre with live radar, and auto-generates IMD-compliant 4-tier color alerts that work even in network-dead zones via an offline-first PWA."
Transition to Next Slide: "Now, let's take a look under the hood to see how this technical engine actually works."
Slide 3 Technical Approach (Under the Hood)
Target Time: 75 Seconds
The Story Angle: An Assembly Line of 4 Clean Steps
Don't drown them in raw math equations; explain the flow as an assembly line that connects satellite data directly to rescue workers.
Spoken Script (Word-for-Word)
"Our technical pipeline is divided into four clean stages:

• Stage 1 is Data Ingestion: We take raw 12 km ensemble forecasts from NCMRWF and compare them against 30 years of climate history from ERA5. If an anomaly exceeds a standardized Z-score of +2.0, the pipeline triggers.
• Stage 2 is 4D Atmospheric Tracking: Instead of flat 2D maps that distort the Earth's poles, we use a Spherical Graph Neural Network on an icosahedral mesh. It tracks wind shear and pressure levels from the ground up to jet stream levels, outputting p10, p50, and p90 uncertainty bounds over 10 days.
• Stage 3 is Generative Downscaling: To sharpen 12 km into 5 km, simple interpolation blurs peaks, and standard AI hallucinates rain. We use a Conditional Diffusion Model paired with a 30-meter elevation map. Crucially, we enforce a mass conservation rule: the AI cannot create water that doesn't exist in the atmospheric column.
• Stage 4 is Automated Alert Dispatch: The output is evaluated against IMD thresholds and packaged into international Common Alerting Protocol (CAP v1.2) format.

Our frontend is built on Next.js 16 and Leaflet for 60-FPS map performance, backed by a lightning-fast FastAPI microservice."
Transition to Next Slide: "A great model on paper means nothing if it cannot survive the harsh reality of a disaster zone. Here is how we ensure feasibility."
Slide 4 Feasibility and Viability (Real-World Resilience)
Target Time: 70 Seconds
The Story Angle: Disarming the Judges' Doubts Before They Ask
Target the 3 big questions judges always have: Big data speed, network collapse in floods, and mountain false alarms.
Spoken Script (Word-for-Word)
"Judges often ask: 'Can this actually run in government workflows, and what happens when disasters strike?' We designed Megh-Drishti around three real-world bottlenecks:

• Challenge 1: Weather files are massive (Gigabytes of data).
Our Solution: We use cloud-native Zarr and Dask chunking. Instead of downloading full multi-gigabyte files, our backend streams only the spatial bounding box needed, delivering inference in just 340 milliseconds on standard GPU servers.

• Challenge 2: Severe storms knock down mobile towers.
Our Solution: If an NDRF field officer loses mobile connectivity, the app does not stop. Built as an Offline-First PWA, the last 72 hours of forecast data remain accessible locally in IndexedDB. If an officer logs a damaged embankment, it queues in the device outbox and auto-syncs the moment 2G or satellite signal flickers back on.

• Challenge 3: Mountain terrains trigger false alarms.
Our Solution: By checking every forecast against 30 years of localized climate records, normal monsoon rain over hilly terrain is not falsely flagged as an anomaly."
Transition to Next Slide: "So, what does all this technology mean for the people on the ground?"
Slide 5 Impact and Benefits (The Human Story)
Target Time: 75 Seconds
The Story Angle: From Panic to Preparation & Livelihoods
Shift focus from code and equations to real lives saved, farmer crop security, and government cost savings.
Spoken Script (Word-for-Word)
"The true success metric of Megh-Drishti is not just accuracy percentages—it is the human and economic impact:

• From Panic to Preparation: Instead of a 3-hour panic response, authorities get 72 to 240 hours of strategic lead time. NDRF teams can pre-position rescue boats in the exact 5 km zones most at risk.
• Fewer False Evacuations: When you issue a blanket warning for an entire district, people suffer 'warning fatigue' and ignore alerts. By narrowing warnings to a 5 km radius, safe neighborhoods remain undisturbed while dangerous zones evacuate safely.
• Protecting Agricultural Livelihood: When farmers know 5 days in advance that their specific taluk faces severe convective rain, they can harvest early, move livestock to higher ground, and clear drainage channels.
• Cost Efficiency: Running full supercomputer numerical simulations at 5 km resolution costs crores in computing power. Our AI downscaler delivers equivalent precision in sub-second inference time using open-source, indigenous models."
